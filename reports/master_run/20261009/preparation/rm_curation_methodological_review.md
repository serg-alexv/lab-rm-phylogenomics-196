## Concrete interpretation limits

1. **HMMER domains are alignment units.** An output domain is a local alignment to a particular profile. A full-protein family profile can produce one alignment spanning several biochemical domains; three asserted enzyme functions do not require three HMMER domains, and one passing domain does not establish three functions. Preserve the actual alignment and assess what that exact profile represents, its coverage and the intact source sequence against characterized family architecture. A genuinely reviewed multifunctional family match can support a predicted fusion without an additional annotation pipeline. The [official HMMER user guide, domain-definition pipeline](https://eddylab.org/software/hmmer/CURRENT/Userguide.pdf) describes this alignment meaning and alternative overlapping envelopes.

2. **Native minima are discovery criteria.** PADLOC TypeI permits two core R/M hits with specificity secondary. DF N6 TypeI permits two of its R/M/S model components. Neither proves all three functions. DF McrBC permits one matching profile despite listing four model entries; those entries must not be described as four biologically required genes or assigned B/C roles by position. See the exact pinned [PADLOC TypeI definition](https://github.com/padlocbio/padloc-db/blob/7f99b47b75e232b111c18626badb9ac32e8e0b5a/sys/RM_type_I.yaml), [DF N6 TypeI definition](https://github.com/mdmparis/defense-finder-models/blob/9b0d07b759f5f73d8c2b9aa3e696f4cdd04c11d2/definitions/RM/RM_Type_I/N6_RM_Type_I.xml), and [DF McrBC definition](https://github.com/mdmparis/defense-finder-models/blob/9b0d07b759f5f73d8c2b9aa3e696f4cdd04c11d2/definitions/RM/McrBC/McrBC.xml).

3. **TypeII completeness must be family-specific.** R/M is a minimum set of functions, not a universal one-R-plus-one-M gene recipe. Some families require additional restriction subunits, two protective methylases, or a separate specificity protein. The contract already requests compatibility review; that review must explicitly cover all obligatory components of the assigned family. A generic nuclease plus neighboring MTase is not proof of cognate target motifs. [Roberts et al. 2003, nomenclature, DOI10.1093/nar/gkg274](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/8762612/A_nomenclature_for_restriction_enzymes.pdf) describes multipart TypeII architectures and overlapping subtype designations.

4. **Fusion alone does not assign IIG.** BcgI and BsaXI are classical IIB examples, also exhibiting other overlapping TypeII properties. In the primary BsaXI study, purified RM and separate S subunits reconstituted activity; the RM fusion alone is therefore insufficient for that family. [Gidwani et al. 2022, DOI10.3389/fmicb.2022.888435](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2022.888435/full). Independently, Furuta et al. explicitly describe a TypeIIG subclass with **“an S subunit ... separate from the RM subunit”** in their Results/Figure5. Their H. pylori study is comparative genomics and predicted recognition diversity, not biochemical verification of those loci. [Furuta et al. 2011, DOI10.1371/journal.pone.0018819](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0018819). Thus a universal one-locus IIG rule is too broad biologically, while a one-locus rule for a specifically supported single-chain native-profile family remains defensible.

5. **Modification dependence alone does not assign IV.** Official nomenclature places DpnI-like fixed-site methyl-dependent enzymes in TypeIIM, while McrBC is TypeIV. No actual DpnI panel case was identified here. Family classification and source architecture must accompany the modification-dependent prediction; this is a boundary condition, not a reported panel misclassification. [Roberts et al. 2003](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/8762612/A_nomenclature_for_restriction_enzymes.pdf).

6. **Prefixes and XML exchangeability are not functional experiments.** `PrrC__EcoprrI` is an MTase exchangeable in the pinned N6 TypeI XML, but is a different profile from `PrrC__PrrC`. Their HMM headers have no descriptive functional annotation and differ in length, training sequence count and thresholds. This does not establish an upstream error or prove that the EcoprrI profile is the PrrC ribonuclease. Its actual seed/provenance/family evidence must determine the bounded role assignment. The separate role witness is useful only when it records a real interpretation rather than repeating a model label.
